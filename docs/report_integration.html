Section 6.4 — Downstream Analyses (All Streams)

Script: 08_integrate_streams.R Output directory: ../outputs/integration/

Overview

08_integrate_streams.R is the final synthesis layer of the prion disease bulk RNA-seq meta-analysis pipeline. It does not re-run any DESeq2 or meta-analysis computation; instead, it reads the final CSV outputs of all three streams and produces a unified cross-stream integration that answers the central biological question of the pipeline:

Which genes are dysregulated in mouse CNS prion disease, conserved in human sCJD, AND detectable in peripheral tissues before terminal disease stage?

These genes are the highest-priority cross-species peripheral biomarker candidates in the pipeline.

6.4.3 Triple-Validated Candidate Analysis

Section 4 | Output: triple_validated_candidates.csv

Tier 1 and Tier 2 genes are the triple-validated candidates — genes that satisfy all three of the following criteria simultaneously:

  1. In the MCPS (Stream 1): Significantly dysregulated across ≥ 3 of 5 CNS Discovery studies, with consistent direction (I² ≤ 75%), padj ≤ 0.05, |LFC| ≥ 0.5.
  2. In the Stream 2 GSEA leading edge (Stream 2): Among the core enrichment genes driving the MCPS gene set enrichment in human sCJD (GSE214373). These are the MCPS genes most strongly ranked in the human DEG list.
  3. First-detected in peripheral tissue (Stream 3): Reach padj ≤ 0.05 in at least one peripheral tissue (whole blood, spleen, or skeletal muscle) at any timepoint in GSE210128.

Result from this run: 0 triple-validated candidates. The Stream 2 GSEA returned 0 results (criterion 2 cannot be evaluated), and peripheral concordance is non-significant in all three tissues. triple_validated_candidates.csv is written as an empty file with correct column headers.

Tier 1 additionally requires that the peripheral LFC direction matches the CNS meta-LFC direction — i.e., the gene is dysregulated in the same direction in both CNS and periphery.

Candidates are ranked by: (1) Tier, (2) earliest peripheral detection wpi (ascending), (3) meta_padj (ascending). This ranking prioritises genes that appear earliest in the periphery, which are the most actionable for early biomarker development.

The output table includes human_lfc and human_padj from Stream 2 (where available), enabling direct comparison of effect sizes across species and tissue compartments.

The output table reports: n (genes with non-NA values in both columns), pearson_r, pearson_p, ci_lower, ci_upper (95% confidence interval from Fisher's z-transformation).

Interpretation note: A positive Pearson r between S1 and S2 indicates that genes upregulated in mouse CNS prion disease tend to also be upregulated in human sCJD — direct cross-species conservation of the transcriptional response. A positive r between S1 and S3 indicates that the CNS signal is partially mirrored in peripheral tissues. Lower r values for peripheral tissues than for human brain are expected, given the tissue-specificity of prion pathology.

Figure 2 (fig2_lfc_scatter_matrix.pdf/png) shows the four primary comparisons (S1 vs S2, S1 vs S3 per tissue) as a 4-panel scatter plot. Triple-validated candidates (Tier 1 + Tier 2) are highlighted as green diamonds with gene labels.

Stage 2 note: Stage 2 reports the mean per-study replication count — the average number of MCPS genes that replicate (same direction, padj ≤ 0.05) in a single Stratum D study. This is computed as mean(replication_rate_pct) × MCPS_size / 100 across the four held-out studies (GSE90977, GSE124932, GSE207251, GSE202275). It is not the union of genes replicated in at least one study. The mean per-study count is a conservative summary: a gene that replicates in 3 of 4 studies contributes 0.75 to the mean rather than 1. The bar chart label reads "mean per study" to make this explicit.

6.4.7 Pathway-Level Human Validation (GSE124571)

Section 9 of nb08 | Output: GSE124571_human_GSEA_vs_consensus_pathways.csv, fig6_human_pathway_validation_bubble.pdf/png

Because the gene-level GSEA (Section 8) returned 0 results due to insufficient MCPS gene set size, a pathway-level validation was performed using GSE124571 — a human sCJD bulk RNA-seq dataset (N=10 sCJD, N=10 control; frontal cortex) that is independent of GSE214373.

Method: GSEA was run on the GSE124571 ranked gene list (all human genes ranked by signed -log10(p) × sign(LFC)) using the 362 Stream 1 consensus pathways as gene sets. This tests whether the same biological pathways enriched in mouse CNS prion disease are also enriched in human sCJD brain.

Result:

  • 314 / 362 consensus pathways significant in human sCJD (padj ≤ 0.05) = 86.7%
  • 311 / 314 significant pathways with NES > 0 (same direction as mouse) = 99.0%
  • Top pathways: Dendritic Cell Differentiation, IL-6 Production, Interferon Gamma Response, Interferon Alpha Response, Myeloid Cell Activation, IL6-JAK-STAT3 Signalling, Adaptive Immune Response, Innate Immune Response, Inflammatory Response, Leukocyte Proliferation

Interpretation: The near-complete conservation of pathway-level enrichment (86.7% of mouse consensus pathways significant in human, 99% in the same direction) provides strong cross-species validation of the Stream 1 biology, even in the absence of gene-level concordance. This is the primary human validation result for the manuscript.

Figure 6 (fig6_human_pathway_validation_bubble.pdf/png) shows the top 15 pathways as a bubble plot (x-axis: NES, bubble size: -log10(padj), colour: gene set database — GO:BP, Hallmark, Reactome).

6.4.8 Pathway Convergence Analysis (Stream 1 Internal)

Section 7 | Output: pathway_convergence.csv, fig5_pathway_convergence.pdf/png

The pathway convergence analysis identifies biological pathways that are robustly dysregulated across multiple streams. Starting from the consensus pathways identified in Stream 1 (enriched in ≥ 3 of 5 CNS Discovery studies by ORA), the analysis checks whether each pathway is also significant in the Stream 1 meta-GSEA.

The output table includes:

  • pathway_id, description, database: pathway identity
  • n_studies_s1: number of Stream 1 Discovery studies in which the pathway is significant (3–5)
  • mean_padj_s1: mean BH-adjusted p-value across Stream 1 studies
  • in_meta_gsea: TRUE if the pathway is also significant (padj ≤ 0.05) in the Stream 1 meta-GSEA
  • convergence_score: n_studies_s1 + 1 if in_meta_gsea (range 3–6)

Pathways with convergence_score = 6 (enriched in all 5 studies AND in meta-GSEA) represent the most robustly dysregulated biology in the pipeline and are the strongest candidates for mechanistic follow-up.

Figure 5 (fig5_pathway_convergence.pdf/png) shows the top 40 pathways as a two-column heatmap: column 1 = n_studies_s1 (blue gradient), column 2 = in_meta_gsea (binary, red/grey).

6.4.10 Statistical and Design Notes

  1. No new statistical tests are run in this script. All p-values and LFC estimates are taken directly from the upstream stream outputs. The integration layer performs joins, correlations, and visualisations only.

  2. Pearson correlations (Section 5) are computed on MCPS genes only (not the full transcriptome). This is intentional: the MCPS is the gene set of interest, and correlating LFCs across streams within this set quantifies how well the CNS prion signature translates to other tissues and species.

  3. Evidence tier assignment uses the Stream 2 GSEA leading edge (not the full MCPS concordance table) as the human validation criterion. The leading edge is a more stringent criterion than simple concordance: it identifies the subset of MCPS genes that are most strongly ranked in the human DEG list and that drive the enrichment score. This reduces the risk of false positives from genes that are nominally concordant but weakly dysregulated in human sCJD.

  4. Replication cascade Stage 2 uses the mean replication rate across Stratum D studies as an estimate. Per-gene replication flags across all Stratum D studies are not aggregated in this script (they would require loading all per-study DESeq2 results). The mean rate is a reasonable summary statistic for reporting but should not be used for gene-level filtering.

  5. Graceful fallback: Every file load uses safe_read(), which returns NULL and prints a warning rather than stopping the script. Downstream sections check is.null() and skip gracefully. This means the script can be run after any subset of streams has completed, producing partial results.

  6. Reproducibility: set.seed(42) is called at the top of the script. No stochastic operations are performed in this script (all randomness is in the upstream streams), but the seed is set for consistency with the rest of the pipeline.

6.4.11 How to Run

# From the scripts/ directory:
Rscript 08_integrate_streams.R

# Or with explicit BASE_DIR (if running from a different directory):
Rscript -e "BASE_DIR <- '/path/to/project'; source('scripts/08_integrate_streams.R')"

The script prints a structured log to stdout and writes integration_summary.txt to outputs/integration/. Expected runtime: < 2 minutes (no DESeq2 or meta-analysis; read-only operations on CSV files).
