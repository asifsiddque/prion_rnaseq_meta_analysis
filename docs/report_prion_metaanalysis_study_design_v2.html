Part 1: Scientific Background and Rationale

1.1 Disease Context

Prion diseases (transmissible spongiform encephalopathies, TSEs) are a group of invariably fatal neurodegenerative disorders caused by the misfolding of the cellular prion protein (PrP^C) into a pathogenic, self-propagating isoform (PrP^Sc). Human forms include sporadic Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (sCJD, ~85% of cases), genetic forms including Fatal Familial Insomnia (FFI, D178N-129M mutation) and Gerstmann-Straussler-Scheinker syndrome (GSS), and acquired forms (variant CJD, kuru, iatrogenic CJD). Animal forms include scrapie (sheep/goat), bovine spongiform encephalopathy (BSE), and chronic wasting disease (CWD).

All prion diseases share a defining clinical feature: a long, clinically silent pre-clinical incubation period (months to decades) followed by rapid, irreversible neurological decline. There are currently no disease-modifying treatments and no validated pre-symptomatic diagnostic biomarkers. This makes the pre-clinical transcriptomic window the most clinically actionable target for biomarker discovery.

1.2 The Unaddressed Research Gap

A systematic review of the current literature reveals four structural gaps that no published study has simultaneously addressed:

Gap 1 — No multi-cohort bulk RNA-seq meta-analysis exists for prion disease. Every published bioinformatics study uses a single GEO dataset in isolation. The Alzheimer's disease field has demonstrated that single-study transcriptomics is insufficient: a 2025 meta-analysis of 24 AD bulk RNA-seq studies found that only 571 genes were reproducibly dysregulated when rigorous meta-analytic methods were applied, compared to thousands reported in individual studies. No equivalent resource exists for prion disease.

Gap 2 — Pre-clinical and terminal transcriptomes have never been jointly meta-analyzed. Sorce et al. (2020) built a preclinical atlas (GSE144738) and Bauer et al. (2022) profiled FFI pre-symptomatically (GSE198063), but these datasets have never been jointly analyzed against terminal-stage cohorts to define a disease-stage-resolved transcriptional trajectory. This is the critical gap for biomarker development: current diagnostic tools are insufficient for pre-symptomatic detection.

Gap 3 — No study has validated mouse-derived prion signatures against human sCJD bulk RNA-seq using rigorous ortholog mapping. The only human bulk RNA-seq sCJD dataset (GSE214373, N=14, MRC Prion Unit at UCL) has not been integrated with the large mouse cohorts in any published meta-analysis.

Gap 4 — The peripheral transcriptome has never been systematically linked to CNS changes. GSE210128 (N=173, blood/muscle/spleen, 8 time points) exists in isolation. No study has asked whether peripheral bulk RNA-seq recapitulates the CNS transcriptomic signature at the pre-clinical stage — a question with direct clinical relevance for non-invasive early diagnosis.

Part 3: Research Questions, Hypotheses, and Objectives

3.1 Primary Research Questions

RQ1 — The Conserved Cross-Stage CNS Signature: What is the minimal, reproducible set of genes that are consistently differentially expressed across both pre-clinical and terminal stages of prion disease in multiple independent mouse brain cohorts, and does this set define a coherent biological program that is directionally conserved in human sporadic CJD brain tissue?

Why this is novel: Requires simultaneous integration of 5 independent studies spanning two disease stages, two prion strains, and multiple brain regions — something no single-dataset study can address. The answer will define the first meta-analytically validated transcriptomic signature of prion neurodegeneration.

RQ2 — The Peripheral Biomarker Question: Which genes or gene modules are concordantly dysregulated in peripheral blood and/or skeletal muscle at the pre-clinical stage of prion disease, and do these peripheral signals mirror the CNS transcriptomic signature identified in RQ1?

Why this is novel: GSE210128 provides 8 longitudinal time points in blood, muscle, and spleen from the same RML6 experiment that also includes a brain sub-cohort, enabling a within-experiment peripheral-CNS comparison that has never been performed.

RQ3 — Disease-Subtype Transcriptomic Divergence: Within the conserved prion signature, which gene modules are shared across all prion disease subtypes (RML scrapie, FFI D178N, sCJD) and which are subtype-specific — and do the subtype-specific modules map to known neuropathological differences between conditions?

Why this is novel: The I² heterogeneity statistic from the random-effects meta-analysis will naturally partition genes into universally conserved (low I²) and subtype-specific (high I², driven by FFI vs. scrapie divergence) categories — a biologically meaningful decomposition that has never been performed.

3.2 Biological Hypothesis

Prion neurodegeneration is driven by a conserved, stage-spanning transcriptomic program organized around three interconnected axes:

  1. Innate immune amplification: Microglial transition to a Disease-Associated Microglia (DAM) state, complement cascade upregulation (C1q, C3, C4b), and interferon-stimulated gene induction — detectable pre-clinically and amplifying through terminal stage.

  2. Proteostasis failure: Sustained activation of the Unfolded Protein Response (UPR) and Integrated Stress Response (ISR), including upregulation of ATF3, DDIT3/CHOP, and downstream translational repression — a mechanism consistent with the known role of eIF2α phosphorylation in prion-induced neurodegeneration.

  3. Synaptic and axonal degeneration: Progressive downregulation of synaptic vesicle genes, neurotransmitter receptor subunits, and cytoskeletal integrity genes — representing the transcriptomic correlate of the spongiform vacuolation and synapse loss observed histologically.

We further hypothesize that a subset of the innate immune axis genes will be detectable in peripheral blood at the pre-clinical stage, providing a candidate non-invasive transcriptomic biomarker panel. The FFI-specific dataset (GSE198063) will share the core neuroinflammatory signature but will exhibit a unique somatostatin neuron vulnerability module distinguishing genetic from sporadic prion disease.

3.3 Statistical Hypotheses

H₀₁ (Meta-analysis null): For any gene g, the true mean log2 fold change across all k CNS Discovery studies is zero under a random-effects model: μ_g = 0 for all g where observed per-study estimates θ̂_ig ~ N(μ_g, σ²_ig + τ²_g), with τ²_g representing between-study variance.

H_A1 (Meta-analysis alternate): A significant subset of genes have μ_g ≠ 0 (meta-padj ≤ 0.05, |meta-log2FC| ≥ 0.5), constituting the Mouse Conserved Prion Signature.

H₀₂ (Heterogeneity null): For any gene g, between-study variance is zero: τ²_g = 0. Tested via Cochran's Q statistic; quantified by I².

H_A2: A significant proportion of MCPS genes show low heterogeneity (I² < 50%), indicating a reproducible cross-study signal rather than study-specific noise.

H₀₃ (Cross-species null): Directional concordance between MCPS orthologs and human sCJD DEGs is no greater than chance: P(same direction in human | significant in mouse) = 0.5

H_A3: Directional concordance significantly exceeds 50% (one-sided binomial p < 0.05), validating cross-species conservation.

H₀₄ (Peripheral-CNS null): Spearman correlation between peripheral blood log2FC and CNS meta-log2FC is zero: ρ_s = 0.

H_A4: A significant positive Spearman correlation exists (ρ > 0, p < 0.05), identifying a peripheral transcriptomic mirror of CNS prion pathology.

Significance thresholds (all analyses):

  • Gene-level: padj ≤ 0.05 (Benjamini-Hochberg FDR), |log2FC| ≥ 0.5 (inclusive)
  • Heterogeneity exclusion: I² > 75% (gene flagged as cohort-specific, excluded from MCPS)
  • Pathway-level: FDR ≤ 0.05

Part 5: Downstream Analysis Pipeline (Corrected and Complete)

5.1 Pipeline Selection: Custom DESeq2 + metafor Framework

Recommendation: Custom framework. Do not use metaXpress as the primary engine.

The key differentiating reason is technical and non-negotiable: tximeta propagates average-transcript-length offset matrices from Salmon's quant.sf files into DESeq2's normalization model. This corrects for transcript-length bias that arises when comparing samples with different isoform usage — a real confounder in disease vs. control comparisons. metaXpress ingests count matrices via mx_load_local() and has no mechanism for this offset correction, meaning it would silently introduce length bias into every DE result.

The valid use case for metaXpress in this project is limited to post-analysis visualization (mx_volcano, mx_forest, mx_heterogeneity_plot) and report generation (mx_report), after the core analysis is complete with the custom framework.

5.2 Complete Analytical Pipeline

The pipeline has THREE completely separate analytical streams that never share count matrices:

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── STREAM 1: CNS META-ANALYSIS (Stratum A — Mouse Brain) ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 1.1 — Import with tximeta (R) For each of the 5 CNS Discovery studies independently: - Locate all quant.sf files for that study - Build a coldata data frame with sample metadata (condition, covariates) - se <- tximeta(coldata) # transcript-level SummarizedExperiment - gse <- summarizeToGene(se) # gene-level with length offsets This produces a SummarizedExperiment with: - counts: integer count matrix (genes × samples) - length: average transcript length matrix (used as offset in DESeq2)

Step 1.2 — Per-study quality filtering - Remove genes with < 10 counts in < 20% of samples within that study - PCA on VST-normalized counts; flag samples > 3 SD from cohort centroid - Document all removals

Step 1.3 — Per-study DESeq2 (run independently for each of the 5 studies) dds <- DESeqDataSet(gse, design = ~ condition) # For studies with known covariates (sex, age, brain region): # design = ~ covariate + condition dds <- DESeq(dds) res <- lfcShrink(dds, coef = "condition_prion_vs_control", type = "apeglm") # Extract: gene_id, log2FoldChange, lfcSE, padj # Save results table to disk; remove dds from memory (rm + gc)

IMPORTANT: Each study is processed sequentially, not simultaneously.
Peak RAM per study: ~2-4 GB for N=186 (GSE149805). Well within 14 GB limit.

Step 1.4 — Random-effects meta-analysis (metafor R package) - Load all 5 per-study results tables (small data frames, negligible RAM) - Restrict to genes present in ≥ 3 of 5 studies (mx_filter_coverage equivalent) - For each gene g: rma_result <- rma(yi = log2FC_vector, sei = lfcSE_vector, method = "DL") # DerSimonian-Laird # Extract: meta-log2FC, meta-SE, Q, I², τ², p-value - Apply Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction across all genes - MCPS = genes with meta-padj ≤ 0.05, |meta-log2FC| ≥ 0.5, I² ≤ 75%

NOTE: ComBat-seq is NOT applied in this stream. In a random-effects
meta-analysis, each study is analyzed independently. Between-study variance
is modeled by τ² — applying ComBat-seq across studies before independent
DESeq2 runs would remove the variance that τ² is designed to estimate,
defeating the purpose of the meta-analytic design.

Step 1.5 — Held-out replication (Stratum D) - Run DESeq2 independently on each of the 7 held-out studies - Test: what fraction of MCPS genes replicate (same direction, padj ≤ 0.05) in each held-out study? - Leave-one-out sensitivity: re-run meta-analysis excluding each Discovery study in turn; confirm MCPS is not driven by any single cohort

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── STREAM 2: HUMAN VALIDATION (Stratum C — Human sCJD) ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 2.1 — Import GSE214373 with tximeta - Human Salmon index (GENCODE v49 / GRCh38) — separate from mouse index - N=14: 5 sCJD post-mortem + 3 sCJD biopsy vs. 9 controls - Note: age is NOT matched (sCJD mean 70.4y vs. control mean 83.7y) Include age as a covariate in the DESeq2 design formula: design = ~ age_group + sample_type + condition This is critical — failure to control for age confounding in a small N=14 dataset would invalidate the human validation entirely.

Step 2.2 — DESeq2 on GSE214373 - Same apeglm shrinkage approach as Stream 1 - Extract human DEG list with log2FC and padj

Step 2.3 — Ortholog mapping (mouse MCPS → human) - Query ENSEMBL BioMart: GRCm39 → GRCh38 one-to-one orthologs - Retain only genes with orthology_confidence = 1 - This reduces the gene universe to ~14,000-16,000 high-confidence 1:1 orthologs - Restrict MCPS to this ortholog-mapped subset

Step 2.4 — Directional concordance test - For each MCPS ortholog, record direction in human sCJD (up/down/NS) - One-sided binomial test: H₀: P(same direction) = 0.5 - Report: concordance rate, binomial p-value, 95% CI - This is a validation test, NOT a pooling operation. Human counts never enter the mouse meta-analysis model.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── STREAM 3: PERIPHERAL ANALYSIS (Stratum B — Mouse Blood/Muscle/Spleen) ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Step 3.1 — Import GSE210128 with tximeta - Separate the 173 samples by tissue: blood, muscle, spleen, brain - For each tissue, build a separate SummarizedExperiment - The brain sub-cohort within GSE210128 serves as internal cross-tissue control

Step 3.2 — Longitudinal DESeq2 per tissue - Time points: 4, 8, 12, 14, 16, 18, 20 wpi, Terminal - Design: ~ time_point + condition + time_point:condition - Use likelihood ratio test (LRT) to identify genes with significant time × condition interaction (i.e., genes that diverge between RML6 and NBH over time) - This captures the temporal dynamics of peripheral transcriptomic changes

Step 3.3 — Peripheral-CNS concordance - Map peripheral DEGs to mouse MCPS (same gene universe) - Spearman correlation: blood log2FC (at each time point) vs. CNS meta-log2FC - Identify the earliest time point at which peripheral-CNS concordance becomes statistically significant (this defines the "transcriptomic detection window" for blood-based biomarkers) - Repeat for muscle and spleen

Part 6: Memory Management for 14 GB RAM (Kaggle/Cloud)

The following strategy ensures the pipeline runs within 14 GB RAM:

  1. Sequential study processing: Never load more than one DESeqDataSet into memory simultaneously. Process, extract results, save to disk, then rm() + gc().

  2. Sparse matrix operations: Use BiocParallel with SerialParam() (single-core) to avoid memory duplication from parallel workers.

  3. tximeta caching: Set BiocFileCache to a persistent directory so reference metadata is not re-downloaded between sessions.

  4. Meta-analysis step is RAM-trivial: metafor operates on results tables (5 data frames of ~20,000 rows each), not count matrices. Total RAM for meta-analysis step: < 500 MB.

  5. GSE210128 (N=173, 4 tissues): Process one tissue at a time. Each tissue sub-matrix (~40-50 samples) requires < 2 GB RAM.

Estimated peak RAM per step:

  • tximeta import (largest study, N=186): ~3-4 GB
  • DESeq2 fitting (N=186): ~4-6 GB
  • Results extraction + rm/gc: returns to ~1 GB
  • metafor meta-analysis: < 500 MB

Part 7: Study Limitations (Required for Publication)

The following limitations must be explicitly stated in the manuscript. Items marked [CONFIRMED] have been verified in the executed pipeline.

  1. [CONFIRMED] GSE144738 dominates the MCPS. Leave-one-out analysis shows that removing GSE144738 reduces MCPS overlap to 16.7% (1/6 genes). The MCPS is substantially dependent on this single study. This must be stated explicitly and the LOO figure included as a supplementary figure.

  2. [CONFIRMED] Human validation cohort is underpowered (N=14). Minimum detectable LFC at 80% power is ≥ 1.50 at median dispersion. All observed MCPS gene LFCs in human are < 0.20. The human dataset cannot detect the effect sizes seen in mouse. This is not evidence of non-replication — it is a power limitation. The age mismatch (sCJD mean 71.6y vs. control 83.3y; p=0.024) is addressed by including age as a covariate (VIF=2.06), but residual confounding cannot be excluded.

  3. [CONFIRMED] Stream 2 GSEA returned 0 results. After ortholog mapping, fewer than 3 MCPS genes had valid rank statistics in the human DEG list. The GSEA leading-edge criterion for evidence tiers (Tier 1–3) cannot be applied. Pathway-level validation (GSE124571) is the primary human evidence layer.

  4. [CONFIRMED] No peripheral concordance at terminal stage. Direction concordance is no better than chance in all three peripheral tissues (binomial p > 0.80). The peripheral signal (Igsf6 at 14 wpi, Fcgr4 at 16 wpi) is direction-discordant — downregulated in periphery vs. upregulated in CNS. This is a genuine negative result for peripheral biomarker concordance, not a pipeline failure.

  5. [CONFIRMED] 0 triple-validated candidates. No gene satisfies all three validation criteria simultaneously. This is the honest result and must be reported as such.

  6. Mouse models do not perfectly recapitulate human sCJD. RML scrapie and FFI D178N knock-in models differ from sporadic human CJD in prion strain, genetic background, and route of inoculation. Cross-species validation (Stream 2) is designed to identify conserved signals despite these differences.

  7. Bulk RNA-seq cannot resolve cell-type-specific effects. Marker-gene deconvolution (Script 09) confirms microglial enrichment as the dominant signal, but cannot fully substitute for single-cell resolution. Findings should be interpreted as tissue-level transcriptomic changes.

  8. GSE210128 peripheral analysis uses a single prion strain (RML6). Whether peripheral transcriptomic changes are strain-specific or universal cannot be determined from this dataset alone.

  9. Replication cascade Stage 2 uses mean per-study rate. The 33% mean replication rate across Stratum D is a conservative summary. GSE202275 shows 100% replication; GSE90977 and GSE207251 show 0%. The high variability reflects genuine biological heterogeneity across prion strains and models.
